What an evidence-mapped questionnaire draft looks like when guessing is prohibited.

The three rows below use placeholders, not a real company, buyer, control, policy, or security claim. They demonstrate the drafting structure only. They are not reusable answers and must not be submitted.

Example 1 — encryption in transit

Illustrative buyer question: Is customer data encrypted in transit?

Draft shape: [COMPANY] states that [DEFINED DATA SCOPE] is encrypted in transit using [CURRENT DOCUMENTED STANDARD]. Evidence: [DOCUMENT ID, SECTION, OWNER, DATE].

Evidence required: A current policy or architecture record that identifies the data scope, standard, control owner, and effective date.

Reviewer action: Confirm the scope and wording against the cited record before submission. If the supplied evidence does not support the statement, the draft says unknown / not evidenced—not “yes.”

Example 2 — access reviews

Illustrative buyer question: Are privileged access rights reviewed periodically?

Draft shape: [COMPANY] reviews [DEFINED ACCESS POPULATION] on [DOCUMENTED CADENCE]. Evidence: [REVIEW RECORD OR POLICY ID, SECTION, OWNER, DATE].

Evidence required: The current access-control policy plus an authorized review record that demonstrates the claimed population and cadence.

Reviewer action: Remove any unsupported population, frequency, or completion claim. A policy without operating evidence does not prove a review occurred.

Example 3 — incident response

Illustrative buyer question: Does the company maintain and test an incident-response plan?

Draft shape: [COMPANY] maintains [PLAN TITLE AND VERSION]. The most recent documented exercise was [AUTHORIZED DATE AND EXERCISE TYPE]. Evidence: [PLAN ID AND EXERCISE RECORD].

Evidence required: A current approved plan and a separate authorized exercise record. The existence of a plan alone does not prove it was tested.

Reviewer action: Confirm dates, scope, and any exceptions. If the exercise record is absent, preserve that gap rather than invent a test.

What the delivered packet adds

  • One row per buyer question, capped at 100 questions per named unit.
  • A draft answer constrained to evidence the company supplied and authorized.
  • An evidence reference or an explicit unknown / not evidenced status.
  • A gap list, reuse index, and a named company-reviewer step.

Clearance Desk does not decide whether a control exists, sign a representation, operate a buyer portal, or promise buyer acceptance. The company reviewer edits, approves, and submits every answer.
